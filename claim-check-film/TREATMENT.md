# Claim Check — treatment

**Logline.** One sentence from a shop page goes into a case file: it is typed, stamped BANNED, and then followed through the tool that finds it, so a viewer can see the sentence, the rule and the article in the same frame.

**Style.** Halftone Dossier (Lemo-Opuscar): cream archive paper, one ink, halftone dots for tone, heavy serif headlines, rubber stamps that slam, dot-wipe cuts. Every printed surface is drawn in code; the only photographs are screenshots of our own running product.

**Length.** 116.0 s, 3840×2160, 30 fps, −14 LUFS.

## Three structures considered

1. **A tour of the interface** — start at the hero, scroll the page, click each section. Rejected: it sells a layout, not a check, and every beat would be "and here is another panel".
2. **Before / after** — a claim as it reads in 2023, then the same claim struck out in 2026. Rejected: the directive has no visual "after"; the product does not rewrite the page, so the second half would be a lie.
3. **A case file of one sentence** (chosen) — the claim is the exhibit; the film proves the finding by opening it up: sentence → rule → citation → the evidence that would make it lawful. The tool appears because it is the thing that reads the sentence, not as a feature list.

Why 3 wins: it is the only structure where the product's output *is* the story, and where the style's apparatus (exhibits, numbers, stamps) carries meaning rather than decoration. It also makes the honest beats — the decoy, the limits — part of the argument instead of a disclaimer at the end.

## Benchmark

- **Learn:** the cold open of a broadcast verification piece — one artefact, held, before any explanation; the way a case-file graphic uses a single accent to point and never to decorate.
- **Don't take:** any channel's furniture, any real newspaper's masthead, any specific stamp design, any existing typeface pairing beyond the licensed families named in `CREDITS`.

## Arc

- **Setup (0–30 s):** a sentence on paper, a stamp, the date the rule applies, the three words the directive names first.
- **Turn (30–77 s):** the page itself; one finding opened into its three parts; six rules; the decoy that proves it is not a keyword grep; the install.
- **Ending (77–116 s):** the privacy promise, the dated run with its limits stated, what it will not do, and the end card — "A check, not a rewrite."

The opening sentence returns as the case number in the HUD on every frame, so the film never leaves the file.

## Camera

Five moves, each with a reason: locked page with the typewriter (attention on the words); a 1.05 push into the product screenshot (scrutiny); staggered card entries rising 26 px (the file assembling); a leader line drawn from the marked phrase to the rule (the argument being made); a dot wipe that covers the cut (a new exhibit pulled from the drawer). No dissolves, no page turns, no zoom that outruns the subject.

## Cue map

92 BPM, one bar = 2.6 s. Sections follow the cuts: sparse pad under the hook and title, bass and hats entering with the directive, full kit through the evidence, thin again for the install, full for the receipts, pad only for the limits, cadence on the end card. Every stamp lands on a beat or on a half-beat, and the picture's cuts sit on bar lines wherever the voice allows.

## Sound design

| Section | Bed | Foley | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | room tone under the typewriter | 26 key ticks, one per character; stamp boom + click | pad alone, cut to silence 2.30–2.68 so the stamp lands alone |
| Title / law | quiet air | paper slide at each cut | pad + marimba motif |
| Evidence | light room | paper, leader-line tick | bass, hats, kick |
| Install / privacy | air | drag whoosh, panel slide | bass only, then snaps |
| Receipts | air | one tick per page square | full kit |
| Limits / end | air | paper | pad, cadence, final stamp |

## Type and titles

Playfair Display 900 for headlines, Inter 400–800 for statements, JetBrains Mono for the HUD, case number and URLs. The caption bar carries the spoken line, one line, with one keyword in gold; the keyword is the word the beat is about. HUD: `CASE 2024/825` and `EXHIBIT nn / 13` top left, a blinking enforcement date top right.

## What the film refuses to do

- It never claims a page is unlawful: the wording is banned *unless* the seller can show recognised excellent environmental performance, and the film says so in the first line.
- The demo page is labelled as synthetic copy on screen, not passed off as a shop.
- The corpus number is stated with its limit in the same frame.
