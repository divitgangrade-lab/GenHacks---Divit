    What happens to your letter

    1. Read. A rules-based parser pulls out medicines, doses, appointments and warnings. No model decides what your letter said.
    2. Rewrite. Each instruction is rendered in short sentences, with clinical shorthand swapped for everyday words.
    3. Check. Every dose, date, drug name and clinical warning in the original must appear in the rewrite. If one is missing, the plan is blocked.
    4. Carry through. Anything the parser does not recognise is kept in the original words rather than guessed at.

    Why it matters: 81% of 1,511 discharge documents sit above the sixth-grade reading level doctors are told to aim for, and 100% do on SMOG. Comprehension errors go on to predict adherence errors. Lowering the reading level cut post-discharge phone calls from 21.9% to 9.0% and readmissions from 1.9% to 0.9% per 100 patients in one study.

    Sample letters are synthetic. No patient data. This is a demonstration, not medical advice: always follow the instructions your own care team gave you.
